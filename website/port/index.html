Queen City List

https://craigslistredesign-theta.vercel.app/

Queen City List addresses the significant usability gap in the current Craigslist interface by implementing a research-driven redesign focused on enhancing user trust and streamlining navigation. Through rigorous usability testing, our team identifies critical friction points such as the overwhelming text-heavy density and the fragmented posting process and replaces them with a modern, card-based visual hierarchy and intuitive faceted search. By prioritizing mobile responsiveness and introducing safer communication patterns, we are transforming a legacy platform into a high-performance marketplace that balances Craigslist’s classic utility with the seamless, secure experience modern users expect.

Main Problem with Craigslist

1. Lack of a Proper Visual Hierarchy

The elements have the same appearance; they all use blue hyperlinks, and the size is the same. There is nothing that stands out and helps users navigate the page by emphasizing the most important information.

2. Zero System Feedback

No validation messages, no preview images, and no breadcrumb trail. As such, users have little to no indication on whether their actions were successful.

3. Cluttered Navigaton

Scores of categories are thrown together into one scrolling list with no organization, visual cues, or any spatial logic

Primary Design Goals

1. Visual Hierarchy
Size, weight, colors, and icons are contrasting to let users immediately
understand which details matter the most.
2. Grid Layout
Removed old columns with links to pictures and replaced them with new
picture grids.
3. Page Consistency
Consistent header, footer, navigation, and design through all five pages so that predictability is guaranteed each time.
4. Recognition Beats Recall
Cards, tags, subcategories, and breadcrumbs are included so that users will never be guessing.
5. Visible System Status
Inline validation, image preview, map feedback, and disabled submission until all required fields of a form submission pass

Design Evaulation

Usability studies conducted on a redesign of the Craigslist prototype by having users (undergraduate students) interact with the prototype and complete tasks. These were accessing a listing, sending a message to a seller, creating a listing, and enabling dark mode on our paper prototype. In our second round, peer testing was done on five users using a think-aloud protocol using our low-fidelity but
interactive Figma prototype.

Conclusion

A familiar design does not necessarily mean it is an intuitive one, because even users who were acquainted with Craigslist had trouble posting ads, and domain familiarity does not equate to a good user interface. Giving feedback to users is very important. All of the users who took part in phase two got stuck at some point due to lack of hover state messages, inline error messages, and progress indication. The low fidelity paper prototyping can show us navigation problems, but the high fidelity showed us interaction problems. Both phases were needed in the process and exposed unique issues to us.
